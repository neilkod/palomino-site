Palomino › Wildlife culling

A good morning in the field leaves a thousand frames of the same bird.

Palomino is a Mac app that finds the sharpest frame in each burst and gets your keepers to your editor, so the card is clear before the next early start.

Wildlife bursts run long, and most frames differ by a wingbeat or a turn of the head. Palomino groups each burst, measures sharpness at the animal's eye when it can find one, and crowns the best frame. You check it, keep it, and reject the rest of the burst with one shortcut.

Palomino is free to download and exports up to 50 photos per session. Palomino Pro removes the limit for a one-time purchase of $29.99.

It requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Back from the field, in Palomino

Open the card

Point Palomino at the card or at the folder you copied it to. Previews come from the JPEG your camera embedded in each RAW file, so a long morning of bursts is ready to look through as soon as the folder opens.

Sharpness measured at the eye

When Palomino finds the subject's eye across a burst, it measures sharpness there instead of across the whole frame. A frame where autofocus caught a branch in front of the animal no longer wins, and the crown stays on the same animal when a second one is in the shot.

Read each burst best first

Press R to lay out every burst in rank order, best frame first. Compare puts two frames side by side with their zoom linked, so you see feather and fur detail in both at once, and the reject-others shortcut clears the rest of the burst in two keys.

Send the keepers

Export to Folder sends your picks or your top-rated frames to a folder, and on the same drive it lands in well under a second. Ratings travel in standard XMP sidecar files, so your editor sees the stars you gave.

Check focus where it matters

A sharp feather tip does not help if the eye is soft. Press Z to zoom to 300 percent, centered on the eyes when Palomino has found them, and the zoom stays on the eyes as you step through the burst. Press ⌘F for the focus mask, which outlines the sharp edges in green. The mask works inside Compare too, so you can see which frame of a burst put the focus on the eye.

Frequently asked questions

Does Palomino find the eye on animals?

Palomino locates eyes on people and on many animals, including dogs, cats, foxes, and deer, and measures sharpness at the eye across the burst. Birds and insects have no eye model on macOS today, so a bird burst is ranked by overall sharpness.

Can Palomino keep up with a long burst?

Yes. Palomino shows each frame from the JPEG preview your camera embedded in the RAW file, so the next frame appears the moment you press the arrow key, and frames shot within half a second of each other stay in one burst even when the subject is small in the frame.

Is Palomino Pro a subscription?

Palomino Pro is a one-time $29.99 purchase through the Mac App Store. You pay once, with no monthly or yearly charge.

Will my ratings show up in my editor?

Palomino writes ratings and color labels to standard XMP sidecar files, so any editor that reads XMP picks them up when you import the folder of selects.

Does Palomino pick my photos for me?

Palomino leaves every pick to you. It crowns the sharpest frame in each burst and flags soft frames, and you accept or skip each suggestion with one key.

What do I need to run Palomino?

Palomino requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later and runs on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
