Rating and flagging

Rate and flag every photo with single keystrokes. Palomino advances to the next photo automatically after each action.

Picks and rejects

KeyAction
P or TPick: marks the photo as a keeper
XReject: marks the photo as a reject
UUnflag: clears both pick and reject, returns to neutral

Press P or X a second time on a photo that already has that flag to clear it. Picks and rejects are independent of star ratings, so you can have a 3-star reject or an unrated pick.

You can cull with one hand using the WASD keys. Keep your left hand on one cluster: W picks, S rejects, A goes back, and D advances. Picks and rejects advance automatically, so you can fly through a shoot without leaving the keys. W and S always advance (they ignore ⇧); use ⇧P / ⇧X when you want to flag without moving on.

Star ratings

KeyAction
1–5Set star rating and advance
⇧1–⇧5Set star rating without advancing
0Clear star rating

In-camera star ratings

Some Hasselblad cameras, including the X2D II, let you star-rate photos on the camera itself. Those ratings are stored in the 3FR file in a way most software misses. When a folder opens, Palomino reads them in the background and applies each one to any photo you have not rated yet. Your own ratings always win: a photo you already rated keeps your rating, and a rating you clear stays cleared, even after closing and reopening the folder.

An applied in-camera rating shows slightly dimmed stars until you rate, flag, or label that photo, and the first change you make converts the stars to the standard appearance. To stop importing in-camera ratings, turn off Apply in-camera star ratings in Settings. Turning it off keeps ratings that were already applied.

Color labels

KeyColor
6Red
7Yellow
8Green
9Blue

Press the same key again on a labeled photo to clear the label. Labels auto-advance to the next photo. Hold ⇧ while pressing a label key (⇧6–⇧9) to assign the label without advancing.

The table above shows the Lightroom convention, which is the default. Phocus reverses that mapping: 6 green, 7 yellow, 8 red, and no key for blue. If you work between Palomino and Phocus, choose Color label keys: Phocus in Settings (⌘,) and the label keys, the shortcuts overlay, and the filter prompt all follow that mapping. Under the Phocus convention 9 does nothing, and blue and purple labels remain available from the context menu and filters.

Auto-advance

By default, assigning a rating, flag, or label moves you forward to the next photo. To assign without moving on, useful when you want to look at a photo longer before deciding, hold ⇧ while pressing the key. This works for ratings (⇧1–⇧5), flags (⇧P, ⇧X), and color labels (⇧6–⇧9). You can also turn auto-advance off entirely in Settings (⌘,).

Clearing actions (0, U, pressing the same label key again) never auto-advance. Only assigning a new value moves you forward.

Rating several photos at once

The grid view can select several photos and apply one decision to all of them. While a selection is active, the same keys change their meaning in two ways: they assign the value to every selected photo instead of toggling, and they never auto-advance. See Viewing and Filtering for how to build a selection with ⌘-click, ⇧-click, and ⌘A.

Jump to the next unrated photo

Press N to jump to the next unrated photo, meaning a photo with no stars, no pick or reject flag, and no color label. It moves forward through the photos that match your current filters and wraps to the start, so repeated presses walk every unrated photo that is left.

Undo

Press ⌘Z to undo your recent rating, flag, and label actions. Undo steps back through up to 50 actions, so a rhythm mistake several frames deep is still recoverable. A change applied to a grid selection counts as one action, so a single ⌘Z restores every photo in the batch. Opening a new folder clears the undo history.

I pressed the wrong key and advanced past the photo. Press the left arrow to go back, then make the correction. ⌘Z reverses the assignment itself but does not move you back to the previous photo, so go back to it first if you want to see the frame you changed.

Eyes-closed warning

Palomino runs face detection in the background and flags photos where a subject's eyes may be closed. A small zzz badge appears on the photo in the filmstrip and the grid, and in the status bar below the photo in the main view, so you can catch a blink before you mark a frame as a keeper.

The badge is a hint, not a verdict. When you have looked at a photo and the eyes are fine, press Y, or choose Ignore Closed Eyes from the Photo menu, to hide the badge for that photo. The override is saved with the session, and pressing Y again brings the badge back.
