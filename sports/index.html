Palomino › Sports culling

The game ends at nine, and the editor wants photos by ten.

Palomino is a Mac app that gets a game's bursts down to the frames worth sending, so your selects reach the editor while the result is still news.

A game shot at 20 frames a second fills a card with near-identical frames. Palomino groups every burst, crowns the sharpest frame, and rejects the rest of the burst with one shortcut. Then it copies your picks to a folder for your editor, and the card is ready for the next game.

Palomino is free to download and exports up to 50 photos per session. Palomino Pro removes the limit for a one-time purchase of $29.99.

It requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later and runs on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

After the final whistle, in Palomino

Open the card

Point Palomino at the card or at the folder you copied it to. Previews come from the JPEG your camera embedded in each RAW file, so several thousand frames are ready to look through as soon as the folder opens.

Collapse the bursts

Palomino groups each burst while you work. Turn on Collapse Bursts and the grid shows one tile per burst with a count of its frames. The BMX jam above goes from 441 frames to 36 tiles.

Keep the sharpest frame

A crown marks the sharpest frame in each burst. Compare puts two frames side by side with their zoom linked, and the reject-others shortcut keeps the frame you are on and rejects the rest of the burst in two keys.

Send the selects

Export to Folder sends your picks or your top-rated frames to a folder, and on the same drive it lands in well under a second. Ratings travel in standard XMP sidecar files, so your editor sees the stars you gave.

Check focus before the frame goes out

Arena light and long glass leave little room for a missed frame. Press ⌘F for the focus mask, which outlines the sharp edges in green, and it works inside Compare too, so you can see which frame of a burst caught the player. For a dim rink, Export to Folder can put frames above an ISO you choose into their own folder, so the noisy ones get extra care in your editor.

Frequently asked questions

Can Palomino keep up with a 20 frames per second burst?

Yes. Palomino shows each frame from the JPEG preview your camera embedded in the RAW file, so the next frame appears the moment you press the arrow key. Frames shot within half a second of each other are grouped into one burst by timing alone, so a burst holds together even when the subject is small in the frame.

Which frame gets the crown in a burst?

Palomino measures sharpness across the burst, at the eye when it finds one, and crowns the sharpest frame. In a long burst it can crown up to four more frames that catch a different moment. Click a crown to read why that frame ranked first.

Is Palomino Pro a subscription?

Palomino Pro is a one-time $29.99 purchase through the Mac App Store. You pay once, with no monthly or yearly charge.

Will my ratings show up in my editor?

Palomino writes ratings and color labels to standard XMP sidecar files, so any editor that reads XMP picks them up when you import the folder of selects.

Does Palomino pick my photos for me?

Palomino leaves every pick to you. It crowns the sharpest frame in each burst and flags soft frames, and you accept or skip each suggestion with one key.

What do I need to run Palomino?

Palomino requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later and runs on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
